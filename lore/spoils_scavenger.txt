title: A Scavenger's Notes
book: rotten-brown
---
Third trip out to the heap. The others call it the Spoils. Nobody knows who named it.

You can see it from a long way off. The red fog sits on it the same as on the Machine, and the heap is the wrong shape for a hill. Up close it is worse. It is made of pieces. Each piece is cut clean on every side, as if somebody took a knife to the world and lifted out a block. A slab of meadow with the grass still on it, turned on its side. A corner of a house, window and all. A cube of ice that does not melt. Copper ore in a seam that runs for three paces and stops at a flat cut.

Old Tam says this is where the Machine threw what it did not need. He says Jonas took pieces of the world to build his work and dumped the leftovers in the sea. I thought that was a story until I stood on a square of somebody's kitchen floor fifty feet up the heap.

There are caves inside where the pieces did not fit together. We went in with lamps. Things lie in there that came from somewhere else: a table set for supper, a tree growing down from the roof with its roots in the stone above, a pond frozen solid in a room with no cold in it. There is salvage too. Ingots, gears, the brass parts the Machine men used. That is why we come.

The Devastation has grown all through it. The thorns come up between the pieces and the ground goes grey under your boots. Do not stay overnight. Do not go into the fog alone. Mark the way back.

Tam did not come out of the north cave. We waited a day. I am leaving these notes in this chest in case he comes back for them.
