title: Chronicle of the Hanging Gardens
book: aged-gray
---
The first year. There were nine of us on the pilgrim boat when the sky turned. The sea went wrong for a month, and when it calmed the boat was on the rocks below the spires and the mainland was gone behind a brown haze. Brother Ise counted the stores. We had seed, tools, and the temple bell we were carrying to the coast.

We had no temple to hang it in. We decided to build one.

The second year. The terrace in the middle of the island is flat enough to build on, and the spires around it keep off the wind. We cut posts from the maples and baked roof tiles from the pond clay. Brother Ise said that if we must live at the end of the world, we should make it the most beautiful place in it. Nobody argued.

The fourth year. We began cutting the stairs. Each spire has a stair inside it now, turning up through the rock, and the stone we cut out built the hall's footing. Where a ledge stood out from a spire we carried up soil in baskets and planted it. The rain does the rest. From the sea the spires look green from top to bottom.

The sixth year. Brother Haru strung the first bridge between the two eastern spires, of planks and bamboo rope, and walked it before anyone else would. Now there are bridges between most of them. You can cross the island without touching the ground.

The ninth year. A boat came. The people in it were starving and half mad from the fog, and they had come from the north, past the Machine. They wanted food. We gave them rice and showed them the gardens. They stayed a week. When they left they asked if they could come back with things to trade.

The twelfth year. There are three stalls by the landing now. Sister Rei sells the furniture the brothers make in the winter. The fields east of the terrace feed us and a market besides. The cloth comes from the flax, and the dye from the plants on the ledges. The boats come every season.

The fifteenth year. Five of the nine are buried on the eastern spire. The rest of us are old. The gardens have grown over the paths in places and we no longer cut them back. Brother Ise says the moss on the lanterns is the temple growing into the island, and that it is meant to.

To whoever reads this after us: tend the gardens. Keep the lanterns lit. Trade fairly with anyone who reaches the landing, because everyone who reaches it has crossed the same sea.
