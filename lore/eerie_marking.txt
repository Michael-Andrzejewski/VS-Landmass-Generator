title: On the Marking of the Pits
by: Warden Aldric
book: aged-brickred
---
When a pit is dug and filled, a marker must be raised over it so that it may be found and so that no one digs there again by mistake.

The markers are cut from the black glass that lies round the site of The Machine. The last firing fused the ground there into a stone like obsidian, harder than common rock. We bring it across by barge in rough blocks and shape it here. It takes the chisel poorly, so keep your marks simple. Cut the name of the place or the day into one face, no deeper than a thumb's width.

Each pit is to be dug no less than eight feet deep. Those who can be named are to be coffined. The rest are laid in rows in cloth, as close as we can manage. Their belongings, whatever was found on them or near them (tools, rings, letters, and the like), go into a chest and are buried with them.

When a pit is full and sealed, paint a cross in red ochre on its marker. This means the pit is closed. It must not be opened again for any reason. The ochre will fade in time, so renew it each spring.

Do this faithfully. We owe them that much.
